# Storage: SSD vs. HDD

Storage is where a computer keeps games, applications, photos, videos, and personal files even after it is shut down. Imagine storing your personal items in a chest. When choosing storage for a gaming PC, the two common options are solid-state drives (SSDs) and hard disk drives (HDDs). Both can store files, but they work differently.

![[ssd v hhd.jpg]]
## SSD: speed and more convenient 

Solid-state drives (SSDs) store data electronically using **non-volatile NAND flash memory** chips with no moving mechanical parts. It can load files much faster than a traditional hard drive, which can mean shorter boot times and quicker game loading. NVMe SSDs connect through an M.2 slot on your [[Motherboards-and-Power-Supplies|Motherboard]] and can offer very high transfer speeds. SATA SSDs are another option to consider and are still much faster than many older hard drives.

### HDD: more space for the money

An HDD stores information on spinning disks. It is usually slower than an SSD, but it can give you a large amount of storage at a lower cost. That can make it useful for photos, videos, and games that do not need the fastest loading times.

## Which one should a gamer choose?

For a gaming PC, I would usually make an SSD preferably a NVMe the main drive for Windows System and the games I play most. If you need lots of extra room and want to keep the cost down, I would add an HDD for larger files that doesn't depend so much on speed. 

### Before buying, check this:

- How much space your games and files need. 
- Whether the motherboard has M.2 slots or SATA connections.
- Whether you want one large drive for everything or separate ones for files and programs. 

## Keep your files safe

 A drive can fail, whether it is an SSD or HDD, so having an extra one matters a lot! The best storage setup will be one that gives you enough room, keeps games loading quickly and that can protect files.
