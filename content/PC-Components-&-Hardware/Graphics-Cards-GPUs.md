# Graphics Cards (GPUs)

The graphics card, also known as a GPU is one of the most important parts of a gaming PC. It creates the images that appear on your screen, which includes the lighting, shadows and VFX. If someone wants to play newer games at a higher resolution like 4K or use demanding graphics settings, the GPU can make a major difference.

![[GPU.jpg]]

## Why the GPU matters

A game running at 1080p usually asks less from a graphics card than the same game running at 1440p or 4K. In a lot of gaming, higher graphics settings requires more GPU power. This is why it helps to know what resolution you want to game in and what frame rate you are trying to achieve before you go shopping. 

### Important things to compare

- **VRAM**: Video memory stores textures and other graphics data. The amount needed depends on the game, resolution, and settings. 
- **Cooling and Noise**: Different cards and models have different fan designs and temps.
- **Size**: Some cards can be thick or long, so make sure it fits withing your PC case.
- **Performance**: Look for benchmarks in the games you actually play.

## Making sure your GPU matches the rest of your PC

A graphics card needs a processor that can keep up with it. If the CPU is too limited for a particular game or frame rate target, it may restrict performance which is also known as ==🔴Bottlenecking.== The [[CPUs-and-Processors | CPU & Processors page]] explains why both matter. 

It is also important that you check the [[Motherboards-and-Power-Supplies|Motherboard & PSU]]. The card must fit in the case, receive the appropriate power and connect to the motherboard. 

## Buying without overspending

I would not automatically buy the most expensive GPU available. Especially with how expensive the prices are due to miners and AI. First, I would decide what games will I be playing, and look a the performance test. A midrange GPU can be more than enough for gaming and will leave you for more [[RAM-and-Memory|RAM]] or more [[Storage-SSD-vs-HDD|SSD]] space.
