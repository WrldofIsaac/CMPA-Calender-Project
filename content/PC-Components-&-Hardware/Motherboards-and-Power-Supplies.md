# Motherboards and Power Supplies

The motherboard and power supply do not always get as much attention as the CPU or graphics card, but they are essential of how your PC can turn on and post on your screen. The motherboard is the main board that connects the components. The power supply unit (PSU) takes power from the wall and delivers it to the parts inside the computer.

![[Motherboard.jpg]]
## Motherboard connecting everything

The motherboard provides different slots and connections for the CPU, RAM, GPU, Storage, fans and other things.

### Important motherboard details

- **CPU Socket**: Making sure it matches the CPU
- **RAM Support**: The board must support the memory gen you plan to use. EX: DDR4 or DDR5
- **Expansions Slots**: Check that there is open slots for a GPU and other different cards. 
- **Storage Connection**: Look for SATA and M.2 connections. 
- **Size Factor**: ATX, Micro-ATX, and Mini ITX boards have different sizes, so the case you are using must be compatible with the board. 

The motherboard manual is worth reading because it explains where to install memory, how to connect the PC case buttons, and which slots to use.

## The power supply keeps the PC running

It is important to choose a quality PSU rather than focusing only on a low price or a large wattage number. Research the power connectors required by the GPU and other components, and leave a good amount headroom for the system.

![[PSU.jpg]]
### General Compatibility list

1. Confirm the CPU socket and motherboard support match.
2. Make sure the RAM type is supported.
3. Check the motherboard size correlating to PC case. 
4. Check the PSU wattage and connectors. 
5. Make sure the case has room for the graphics card and cooling.
